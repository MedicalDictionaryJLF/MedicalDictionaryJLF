Key parameters: prolong repolarization and effective refractory period; prolong QT interval; examples: amiodarone, sotalol, dofetilide, ibutilide, dronedarone; main danger: torsades de pointes, except amiodarone has lower torsades risk but many organ toxicities.
Sotalol	Antiarrhythmic class III with class II beta-blocking activity
Key parameters: blocks K+ channels and β receptors; used for selected atrial and ventricular arrhythmias; adverse effects: QT prolongation, torsades de pointes, bradycardia, bronchospasm risk.
Dofetilide	Antiarrhythmic class III; potassium-channel blocker
Key parameters: rhythm control in atrial fibrillation/flutter in selected patients; requires QT and renal monitoring; major risk: torsades de pointes.
Ibutilide	Antiarrhythmic class III; potassium-channel blocker
Key parameters: IV conversion of atrial flutter/fibrillation in selected settings; major risk: QT prolongation and torsades de pointes.
Dronedarone	Antiarrhythmic class III; amiodarone analogue
Key parameters: rhythm control in selected atrial fibrillation patients; less iodine-related toxicity than amiodarone but less effective; contraindicated in severe/decompensated heart failure and permanent AF.
Antiarrhythmic drugs - Class IV	Antiarrhythmic classification; Class IV = non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers
Key parameters: verapamil and diltiazem; slow AV node conduction and reduce heart rate; useful for SVT and AF rate control; adverse effects: bradycardia, AV block, hypotension, constipation especially verapamil.
Antiarrhythmic drugs - Class V	Antiarrhythmic classification; Class V = miscellaneous antiarrhythmics
Key parameters: drugs that do not fit cleanly into Classes I-IV; examples: adenosine, digoxin, magnesium sulfate, atropine; mechanisms vary, because apparently one tidy classification was too merciful.
Adenosine	Antiarrhythmic class V; endogenous nucleoside
Key parameters: transiently blocks AV nodal conduction via A1 receptors; first-line acute termination of many regular narrow-complex SVTs; very short half-life; adverse effects: flushing, chest discomfort, dyspnea, brief asystole feeling.
Magnesium sulfate	Antiarrhythmic class V; electrolyte therapy
Key parameters: treatment of torsades de pointes and arrhythmias related to hypomagnesemia; also used in eclampsia; adverse effects: hypotension, flushing, loss of reflexes/respiratory depression in toxicity.
Atropine as antiarrhythmic	Antiarrhythmic class V use; antimuscarinic vagolytic
Key parameters: blocks muscarinic receptors and increases SA/AV node activity; used for symptomatic bradycardia; adverse effects: tachycardia, dry mouth, urinary retention, confusion.
Digoxin as antiarrhythmic	Antiarrhythmic class V use; cardiac glycoside with vagotonic AV-node effect
Key parameters: slows AV nodal conduction and helps rate control in atrial fibrillation, especially with heart failure; narrow therapeutic index; toxicity causes arrhythmias, GI symptoms, visual disturbances.
Antiarrhythmic drugs - Class VI	Antiarrhythmic classification; Class VI = connexin/gap-junction modifiers
Key parameters: target cell-to-cell electrical coupling through connexins; mostly experimental or not routine clinical antiarrhythmics; example concept: gap-junction enhancers such as rotigaptide; exam note: know the target more than a standard drug list.
Rotigaptide	Experimental antiarrhythmic concept; Class VI connexin/gap-junction enhancer
Key parameters: investigational gap-junction modifier; aims to improve intercellular conduction and reduce re-entry substrate; not a routine clinical antiarrhythmic.
Antiarrhythmic drugs - Class VII	Antiarrhythmic classification; Class VII = upstream/remodeling-target drugs
Key parameters: target long-term arrhythmogenic remodeling rather than acute ion-channel blockade; examples often include ACE inhibitors, ARBs, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, statins, and anti-inflammatory/antifibrotic approaches; clinically relevant as prevention/support, not acute conversion.
Upstream antiarrhythmic therapy	Antiarrhythmic concept; Class VII remodeling prevention
Key parameters: reduces atrial/ventricular remodeling drivers such as fibrosis, stretch, inflammation, and neurohormonal activation; examples: ACE inhibitors/ARBs, aldosterone antagonists, statins in selected contexts; supportive rather than classic acute antiarrhythmic therapy.
ARNI (angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor)	Heart failure drug class; ARB + neprilysin inhibitor combination
Key parameters: combines RAAS blockade with increased natriuretic peptides; prototype is sacubitril/valsartan; used especially in chronic HFrEF; adverse effects: hypotension, hyperkalemia, renal function worsening, angioedema risk.
Sacubitril/valsartan	ARNI; angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor
Key parameters: sacubitril inhibits neprilysin and valsartan blocks AT1 receptors; used in chronic HFrEF to reduce morbidity/mortality; do not combine with ACE inhibitors; requires ACE inhibitor washout because of angioedema risk.
Sacubitril	Neprilysin inhibitor component of ARNI
Key parameters: increases natriuretic peptides by inhibiting their breakdown; promotes natriuresis, vasodilation, and reduced remodeling signals; clinically used only in fixed combination with valsartan.
ARNI contraindications and cautions	Heart failure safety card
Key parameters: contraindicated with ACE inhibitors, pregnancy, history of angioedema related to ACEi/ARB/ARNI, and significant hyperkalemia; caution in renal impairment, hypotension, and bilateral renal artery stenosis.
SGLT2 inhibitors in chronic heart failure	Heart failure drug class; sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors
Key parameters: reduce heart failure hospitalization and improve outcomes in HFrEF and many HFpEF patients; mechanisms include osmotic diuresis, natriuresis, renal/metabolic benefits; adverse effects: genital infections, volume depletion, euglycemic ketoacidosis risk.
Dapagliflozin	SGLT2 inhibitor; heart failure and antidiabetic drug
Key parameters: promotes glucosuria and natriuresis; used in diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and chronic heart failure; adverse effects: genital mycotic infections, dehydration, hypotension, euglycemic ketoacidosis.
Empagliflozin in heart failure	SGLT2 inhibitor; heart failure disease-modifying drug
Key parameters: useful in chronic heart failure even without diabetes; causes glucosuria/natriuresis; adverse effects: genital infections, volume depletion, rare euglycemic ketoacidosis.
Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists in heart failure	Heart failure drug class; aldosterone receptor blockers
Key parameters: reduce aldosterone-mediated sodium retention, potassium loss, fibrosis, and remodeling; examples: spironolactone, eplerenone; adverse effect: hyperkalemia; monitor renal function and potassium.
Eplerenone	Potassium-sparing diuretic; selective mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist
Key parameters: used in HFrEF and post-MI LV dysfunction; less gynecomastia than spironolactone; adverse effects: hyperkalemia, renal function worsening.
Hydralazine-isosorbide dinitrate combination	Heart failure vasodilator combination
Key parameters: hydralazine reduces afterload and isosorbide dinitrate reduces preload; option in HFrEF when ACEi/ARB/ARNI not tolerated or as add-on in selected patients; adverse effects: headache, hypotension, reflex tachycardia.
Isosorbide dinitrate	Organic nitrate; antianginal and heart failure preload reducer
Key parameters: releases NO and increases cGMP; venodilation reduces preload; used in angina and with hydralazine in selected HFrEF; adverse effects: headache, hypotension, tolerance; contraindicated with PDE5 inhibitors.
Hydralazine	Direct arterial vasodilator; antihypertensive/heart failure add-on
Key parameters: relaxes arteriolar smooth muscle and lowers afterload; used in pregnancy hypertension, hypertensive urgency/emergency in selected cases, and with nitrates in HFrEF; adverse effects: reflex tachycardia, fluid retention, headache, drug-induced lupus.
Central alpha-2 agonists and imidazoline agonists	Centrally acting antihypertensive class
Key parameters: reduce sympathetic outflow from the CNS; examples: clonidine, methyldopa, moxonidine; adverse effects: sedation, dry mouth, bradycardia; clonidine withdrawal can cause rebound hypertension.
Clonidine	Central α2-adrenergic agonist; antihypertensive
Key parameters: decreases sympathetic outflow; used in resistant hypertension and some withdrawal syndromes; adverse effects: sedation, dry mouth, bradycardia, rebound hypertension if stopped abruptly.
Methyldopa	Central α2-adrenergic agonist; antihypertensive
Key parameters: classic antihypertensive in pregnancy; converted to α-methylnoradrenaline; adverse effects: sedation, hyperprolactinemia, hemolytic anemia, hepatotoxicity.
Moxonidine	Imidazoline I1 receptor agonist; centrally acting antihypertensive
Key parameters: reduces sympathetic tone; used in hypertension in some settings; adverse effects: dry mouth, dizziness, fatigue, bradycardia.
Direct arterial vasodilators	Antihypertensive class; vascular smooth muscle relaxants
Key parameters: reduce systemic vascular resistance by arteriolar dilation; examples: hydralazine and minoxidil; adverse effects: reflex tachycardia and fluid retention, so often combined with beta-blocker/diuretic when appropriate.
Minoxidil	Direct arterial vasodilator; K+ channel opener
Key parameters: potent antihypertensive for resistant hypertension; also used topically for alopecia; adverse effects: fluid retention, reflex tachycardia, hypertrichosis, pericardial effusion rarely.
Hypertensive emergency drugs	Acute blood pressure control class; mainly IV agents
Key parameters: used for severe hypertension with acute target-organ damage; reduce BP in a controlled way; examples: labetalol, esmolol, nicardipine, nitroglycerin, urapidil; choice depends on clinical scenario.
Labetalol	Beta-blocker with α1-blocking activity; IV/oral antihypertensive
Key parameters: blocks β1/β2 and α1 receptors; useful in hypertensive emergency and pregnancy-related severe hypertension; adverse effects: hypotension, bradycardia, bronchospasm risk.
Nicardipine	Dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker; IV antihypertensive
Key parameters: arterial vasodilator used in hypertensive emergency and neurologic BP control; adverse effects: headache, flushing, reflex tachycardia, edema.
Urapidil	Antihypertensive; α1 antagonist with central serotonergic sympatholytic effect
Key parameters: IV option for hypertensive crisis in some countries; lowers peripheral resistance with less reflex tachycardia; adverse effects: dizziness, headache, hypotension.
P2Y12 inhibitors	Antiplatelet class; ADP receptor antagonists
Key parameters: inhibit platelet activation through P2Y12 blockade; examples: clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor; used in ACS and after stenting; major adverse effect: bleeding.
Prasugrel	Antiplatelet drug; irreversible P2Y12 ADP receptor antagonist
Key parameters: prodrug; stronger platelet inhibition than clopidogrel; used in ACS/PCI; adverse effect: bleeding; avoid in prior stroke/TIA.
Ticagrelor	Antiplatelet drug; reversible P2Y12 ADP receptor antagonist
Key parameters: not a prodrug; used in ACS and after stenting; adverse effects: bleeding, dyspnea, bradyarrhythmias; interacts with strong CYP3A modifiers.
GPIIb/IIIa inhibitors	Antiplatelet class; final common pathway platelet aggregation blockers
Key parameters: block fibrinogen-mediated platelet cross-linking; examples: abciximab, eptifibatide, tirofiban; mainly IV use in selected ACS/PCI settings; major risks: bleeding and thrombocytopenia.
Abciximab	GPIIb/IIIa inhibitor; monoclonal antibody fragment antiplatelet
Key parameters: blocks platelet GPIIb/IIIa receptors; IV use in selected PCI/ACS settings; adverse effects: bleeding, thrombocytopenia.
Eptifibatide	GPIIb/IIIa inhibitor; cyclic peptide antiplatelet
Key parameters: IV reversible platelet aggregation inhibitor; used in selected ACS/PCI settings; adverse effects: bleeding, thrombocytopenia; renal dosing matters.
Tirofiban	GPIIb/IIIa inhibitor; non-peptide antiplatelet
Key parameters: IV reversible GPIIb/IIIa blocker; selected ACS/PCI use; adverse effects: bleeding, thrombocytopenia; adjust in renal impairment.
Anticoagulant reversal agents	Hemostasis/antidote category
Key parameters: reverse or bypass anticoagulant effect in major bleeding or urgent procedures; examples: protamine for heparin, vitamin K/PCC for warfarin, idarucizumab for dabigatran, andexanet alfa for factor Xa inhibitors where available.
Protamine sulfate	Antidote; heparin reversal agent
Key parameters: positively charged protein that binds negatively charged heparin; reverses unfractionated heparin best and partially reverses LMWH; adverse effects: hypotension, anaphylactoid reactions.
Vitamin K (phytomenadione)	Antidote; warfarin reversal support
Key parameters: restores hepatic synthesis of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors; used for warfarin over-anticoagulation/bleeding; slow compared with PCC; effect depends on liver synthetic function.
Prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC)	Anticoagulant reversal product; clotting factor concentrate
Key parameters: rapidly replaces vitamin K-dependent factors, especially for serious warfarin-associated bleeding; often combined with vitamin K; major risk: thrombosis.
Idarucizumab	Antidote; dabigatran reversal monoclonal antibody fragment
Key parameters: binds dabigatran and rapidly reverses direct thrombin inhibition; used for life-threatening bleeding or urgent surgery; thrombosis risk after reversal if anticoagulation not restarted when appropriate.
Andexanet alfa	Antidote; factor Xa inhibitor reversal agent
Key parameters: recombinant modified factor Xa decoy; reverses apixaban/rivaroxaban in selected severe bleeding settings where available; adverse effects: thrombosis risk, infusion reactions.
Ezetimibe	Hypolipidemic drug; cholesterol absorption inhibitor
Key parameters: inhibits NPC1L1 cholesterol uptake in the intestine; lowers LDL; used alone or with statins; adverse effects: GI upset, liver enzyme elevation especially with statins.
PCSK9 inhibitors	Hypolipidemic biologic class; LDL receptor recycling enhancers
Key parameters: monoclonal antibodies against PCSK9 increase hepatic LDL receptor recycling and markedly lower LDL; examples: evolocumab, alirocumab; adverse effects: injection-site reactions, flu-like symptoms.
Evolocumab	PCSK9 inhibitor; monoclonal antibody hypolipidemic
Key parameters: greatly lowers LDL by increasing LDL receptor recycling; used in familial hypercholesterolemia or high-risk ASCVD when LDL remains high; adverse effects: injection-site reactions.
Alirocumab	PCSK9 inhibitor; monoclonal antibody hypolipidemic
Key parameters: lowers LDL by inhibiting PCSK9; used in high-risk dyslipidemia and familial hypercholesterolemia; adverse effects: injection-site reactions, nasopharyngitis.
Fibrates	Hypolipidemic class; PPAR-α agonists
Key parameters: mainly lower triglycerides and raise HDL modestly; examples: fenofibrate, gemfibrozil; adverse effects: myopathy risk especially with statins, gallstones, liver enzyme elevation.
Fenofibrate	Fibrate hypolipidemic; PPAR-α agonist
Key parameters: lowers triglycerides by increasing lipoprotein lipase activity; used in hypertriglyceridemia; adverse effects: myopathy, gallstones, liver enzyme elevation.
Bile acid sequestrants	Hypolipidemic class; intestinal bile acid binders
Key parameters: bind bile acids in gut and increase hepatic LDL receptor expression; examples: cholestyramine, colestipol, colesevelam; adverse effects: constipation, bloating, reduced absorption of other drugs/vitamins.
Cholestyramine	Bile acid sequestrant; hypolipidemic resin
Key parameters: lowers LDL by binding bile acids; also useful for some bile acid diarrhea and pruritus from cholestasis; adverse effects: constipation, bloating, drug binding interactions.
PDE5 inhibitors in pulmonary arterial hypertension	Pulmonary hypertension vasodilator class
Key parameters: inhibit PDE5 and increase cGMP-mediated pulmonary vasodilation; example: sildenafil; adverse effects: headache, flushing, hypotension; contraindicated with nitrates because of severe hypotension risk.
Sildenafil	PDE5 inhibitor; pulmonary arterial hypertension and erectile dysfunction drug
Key parameters: increases cGMP by inhibiting PDE5; causes pulmonary and systemic vasodilation; adverse effects: headache, flushing, hypotension, visual disturbance; contraindicated with nitrates.
ADME	Absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion; the core pharmacokinetic sequence determining drug concentration over time.
Absorption	Movement of drug from administration site into blood; affected by route, solubility, pH, blood flow, formulation, food, and transporters.
Bioavailability	Fraction of administered dose reaching systemic circulation unchanged; IV is 100%; oral drugs may lose bioavailability through incomplete absorption and first-pass metabolism.
First-pass metabolism	Presystemic metabolism mainly in gut wall and liver before drug reaches systemic circulation; lowers oral bioavailability.
Routes of administration	Enteral: oral, sublingual, rectal. Parenteral: IV, IM, SC, inhaled, transdermal. Route affects onset, bioavailability, and safety.
Distribution	Movement of drug from blood into tissues; influenced by perfusion, plasma protein binding, lipid solubility, barriers, and volume of distribution.
Volume of distribution (Vd)	Apparent volume relating amount of drug in body to plasma concentration; high Vd suggests tissue binding/distribution, low Vd suggests blood/plasma confinement.
Plasma protein binding	Only free drug is pharmacologically active and filtered; displacement interactions matter most for highly bound narrow-therapeutic-index drugs.
Clearance	Volume of plasma cleared of drug per unit time; mainly renal and hepatic; determines maintenance dose.
Half-life	Time for plasma drug concentration to fall by 50%; determines dosing interval and time to steady state.
Steady state	Drug input equals elimination; usually reached after about 4-5 half-lives with repeated dosing.
Loading dose	Initial larger dose used to rapidly reach target concentration; depends mainly on volume of distribution and target concentration.
Maintenance dose	Dose needed to maintain steady-state concentration; depends mainly on clearance, target concentration, dosing interval, and bioavailability.
Therapeutic index	Safety margin between effective and toxic doses; narrow therapeutic index drugs require monitoring, e.g. warfarin, digoxin, lithium.
Potency vs efficacy	Potency = dose needed for effect; efficacy = maximal effect. More potent does not necessarily mean more effective.
Full agonist	Binds receptor and produces maximal response when enough receptors are occupied.
Partial agonist	Activates receptor but produces lower maximal response than full agonist; can antagonize a full agonist in the same system.
Competitive antagonist	Reversibly competes with agonist at receptor; effect can be overcome by increasing agonist concentration; shifts dose-response curve right.
Noncompetitive antagonist	Reduces maximal response; effect cannot be fully overcome by increasing agonist concentration.
Type A adverse drug reaction	Augmented, dose-related, predictable from pharmacology; common example: bleeding from anticoagulants.
Type B adverse drug reaction	Bizarre/idiosyncratic or immune-mediated, not clearly dose-related; example: drug allergy or anaphylaxis.
Pharmacovigilance	Detection, assessment, understanding, and prevention of adverse drug effects after drugs are marketed.
Pharmacokinetic interactions	Interactions affecting absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion; examples: enzyme inhibition/induction, altered renal clearance.
Pharmacodynamic interactions	Interactions at effect level without changing concentration; examples: additive CNS depression, additive bleeding risk, antagonistic receptor effects.
Drug use in elderly patients	Higher risk from reduced renal/hepatic function, polypharmacy, altered body composition, and sensitivity to CNS/anticholinergic drugs; start low, go slow.
Drug effects on fetus	Drugs can cause teratogenicity, fetal toxicity, or neonatal toxicity; risk depends on drug, dose, timing, and placental transfer.
Prescription basics	A prescription should clearly state patient, drug name, strength, dosage form, route, dose, frequency, duration, quantity, instructions, date, and prescriber.
Benzodiazepines - class	Positive allosteric modulators of GABA-A receptors; increase frequency of chloride channel opening; anxiolytic, hypnotic, anticonvulsant, muscle relaxant.
Barbiturates - class	GABA-A enhancers that prolong chloride channel opening; anticonvulsant/anesthetic uses; dangerous overdose with CNS and respiratory depression.
Z-drugs - class	Hypnotics acting at benzodiazepine site on GABA-A, relatively sleep-selective; examples: zolpidem, zopiclone; used for insomnia.
SSRIs - class	Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors; first-line for depression/anxiety; adverse effects include GI upset, sexual dysfunction, serotonin syndrome risk.
SNRIs - class	Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors; examples: venlafaxine, duloxetine; used in depression, anxiety, neuropathic pain.
Tricyclic antidepressants - class	Inhibit serotonin/noradrenaline reuptake; anticholinergic, sedating, and cardiotoxic in overdose; examples: amitriptyline, imipramine.
MAO inhibitors - class	Increase monoamines by inhibiting monoamine oxidase; risk of hypertensive crisis with tyramine and serotonin syndrome with serotonergic drugs.
Typical antipsychotics - class	Mainly D2 antagonists; useful in psychosis/agitation; adverse effects: extrapyramidal symptoms, hyperprolactinemia, QT prolongation, neuroleptic malignant syndrome.
Atypical antipsychotics - class	D2 and 5-HT2A antagonism/partial agonism patterns; lower EPS generally, but more metabolic effects; examples: clozapine, risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine.
Antiepileptic sodium-channel blockers	Stabilize inactive Na+ channels and reduce high-frequency neuronal firing; examples: phenytoin, carbamazepine, lamotrigine.
GABA-enhancing antiepileptics	Increase inhibitory neurotransmission; examples include benzodiazepines, phenobarbital, valproate, tiagabine, vigabatrin.
SV2A modulators	Antiepileptics acting on synaptic vesicle protein SV2A; prototype: levetiracetam; few interactions but mood/irritability possible.
Status epilepticus treatment	Emergency treatment: benzodiazepine first, then loading antiepileptic such as levetiracetam/phenytoin/valproate; refractory cases may need anesthetic coma.
Opioid agonists - class	Mu-opioid receptor agonists causing analgesia, sedation, miosis, respiratory depression, constipation, tolerance, and dependence; overdose antidote: naloxone.
Opioid antagonists - class	Block opioid receptors; naloxone reverses overdose acutely, naltrexone is longer acting and used in dependence treatment.
NSAIDs - class	Inhibit cyclooxygenase and reduce prostaglandins; analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory; adverse effects: GI bleeding, renal injury, bronchospasm, CV risk.
Paracetamol poisoning	Toxic metabolite depletes glutathione and causes hepatic necrosis; antidote is N-acetylcysteine, best early but useful later too.
General anesthetics - class	Produce reversible unconsciousness/amnesia/immobility; examples: propofol, ketamine, sevoflurane; risks include hypotension, respiratory depression, malignant hyperthermia with volatile agents.
Local anesthetics - class	Block voltage-gated sodium channels; prevent nerve impulse conduction; toxicity includes CNS symptoms, seizures, arrhythmias; severe toxicity treated with lipid emulsion.
Beta-blocker overdose	Causes bradycardia, hypotension, AV block, bronchospasm, hypoglycemia; treatment may include atropine, glucagon, vasopressors, high-dose insulin therapy.
Calcium-channel blocker overdose	Causes hypotension, bradycardia/AV block especially verapamil/diltiazem, hyperglycemia; treatment includes calcium, vasopressors, high-dose insulin-glucose therapy.
Digoxin toxicity	Symptoms: GI upset, confusion, visual changes, arrhythmias, hyperkalemia in acute poisoning; severe toxicity treated with digoxin immune Fab.
Local anesthetic systemic toxicity	CNS excitation/seizures followed by cardiovascular collapse; treat with airway support, benzodiazepines for seizures, and IV lipid emulsion.
Cyanide poisoning	Blocks mitochondrial cytochrome oxidase causing cellular hypoxia and lactic acidosis; antidote: hydroxocobalamin.
Methemoglobinemia	Oxidized hemoglobin cannot carry oxygen effectively; cyanosis with low pulse oximetry; antidote: methylene blue.
Iron poisoning	Causes GI corrosion, shock, metabolic acidosis, hepatic failure; antidote/chelator: deferoxamine.
Beta-lactam antibiotics - class	Inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding PBPs; bactericidal; main groups: penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems, monobactams.
Natural penicillins	Penicillin G/V; good for many Gram-positive organisms and spirochetes; beta-lactamase sensitive; allergy risk.
Aminopenicillins	Amoxicillin/ampicillin; broader Gram-negative coverage than penicillin G; often combined with beta-lactamase inhibitor.
Antistaphylococcal penicillins	Oxacillin/flucloxacillin/nafcillin; beta-lactamase-resistant; used for MSSA, not MRSA.
Antipseudomonal penicillins	Piperacillin/ticarcillin; cover Pseudomonas; usually combined with tazobactam/clavulanate in severe infections.
Beta-lactamase inhibitors	Clavulanate, sulbactam, tazobactam; inhibit many beta-lactamases and expand beta-lactam spectrum; little antibacterial activity alone.
Cephalosporin generations	1st gen mainly Gram-positive; later generations add Gram-negative/CNS coverage; cefepime covers Pseudomonas; ceftaroline covers MRSA.
Carbapenems	Very broad beta-lactams; examples: meropenem, imipenem; severe resistant infections; seizure risk especially imipenem.
Monobactams	Aztreonam; active against aerobic Gram-negative rods including Pseudomonas; minimal cross-allergy with penicillins.
Vancomycin	Glycopeptide inhibiting cell wall synthesis; used for MRSA and severe Gram-positive infections; adverse effects: nephrotoxicity, red man syndrome.
Linezolid	Oxazolidinone inhibiting 50S initiation complex; active against MRSA/VRE; adverse effects: thrombocytopenia, serotonin syndrome risk.
Cotrimoxazole	Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole; blocks folate synthesis sequentially; uses include UTI, Pneumocystis jirovecii, some MRSA; adverse effects include rash, marrow suppression, hyperkalemia.
Nitrofurantoin	Urinary antiseptic for uncomplicated lower UTI; not for pyelonephritis; adverse effects include GI upset, pulmonary toxicity rarely, hemolysis in G6PD deficiency.
Macrolides - class	50S protein synthesis inhibitors; cover atypical respiratory pathogens; examples: azithromycin, clarithromycin; adverse effects: QT prolongation, GI upset.
Tetracyclines - class	30S inhibitors; examples: doxycycline; cover atypicals, rickettsiae, chlamydia, acne; adverse effects: photosensitivity, teeth/bone effects; avoid pregnancy/children.
Aminoglycosides - class	30S inhibitors with concentration-dependent killing; Gram-negative aerobes; adverse effects: nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, neuromuscular blockade.
Fluoroquinolones - class	Inhibit DNA gyrase/topoisomerase; adverse effects: tendinopathy, QT prolongation, CNS effects, dysglycemia; avoid pregnancy/children if possible.
Anti-herpes antivirals	Acyclovir/valacyclovir; guanosine analogues activated by viral thymidine kinase; inhibit viral DNA polymerase; HSV/VZV; nephrotoxicity risk.
HIV drug classes overview	Main classes: NRTIs, NNRTIs, protease inhibitors, integrase inhibitors, entry/fusion inhibitors; usually combined to prevent resistance.
NRTIs	Nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors; backbone of many HIV regimens; examples: tenofovir, emtricitabine, lamivudine, abacavir.
NNRTIs	Non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors; examples: efavirenz, nevirapine, rilpivirine; rash and CYP interactions are important.
HIV protease inhibitors	Prevent viral protein processing; examples: ritonavir, darunavir; adverse effects: metabolic syndrome, lipodystrophy, many CYP interactions.
Integrase inhibitors	Block HIV integration into host genome; examples: dolutegravir, raltegravir, bictegravir; generally well tolerated.
Hepatitis antivirals overview	HBV: tenofovir, entecavir. HCV: direct-acting antivirals such as sofosbuvir-based regimens.
Azole antifungals - class	Inhibit ergosterol synthesis via 14-alpha-demethylase; examples: fluconazole, voriconazole; adverse effects: hepatotoxicity, CYP interactions.
Echinocandins	Inhibit fungal beta-glucan cell wall synthesis; examples: caspofungin, micafungin; useful for Candida including invasive disease.
Antimalarials overview	Chloroquine for sensitive malaria, artemisinin combinations for many falciparum cases, primaquine for hypnozoites; resistance and region matter.
Chloroquine	Antimalarial active against sensitive Plasmodium; also rheumatologic use; adverse effects: retinopathy, QT prolongation, pruritus.
Artemisinin combinations	Rapidly acting antimalarials used in combination therapy for falciparum malaria; adverse effects include GI symptoms and rare QT issues depending partner drug.
Primaquine	Kills dormant liver hypnozoites of P. vivax/ovale; can cause hemolysis in G6PD deficiency; test G6PD before use.
Asthma relievers	Rapid bronchodilators for symptoms; main prototype is salbutamol; formoterol can be used as reliever in ICS-formoterol regimens.
Asthma controller drugs	Reduce airway inflammation and exacerbations; inhaled corticosteroids are the core controllers; LABA/LAMA/leukotriene antagonists are add-ons.
LABA drugs	Long-acting beta2 agonists such as salmeterol and formoterol; maintenance therapy in asthma/COPD; in asthma should be used with inhaled corticosteroid.
Inhaled corticosteroids	Budesonide/fluticasone; first-line anti-inflammatory controllers in persistent asthma; adverse effects: oral candidiasis, dysphonia.
Montelukast	Leukotriene receptor antagonist; used in asthma/allergic rhinitis; less effective than inhaled corticosteroids; neuropsychiatric adverse effects possible.
Omalizumab	Anti-IgE monoclonal antibody for severe allergic asthma/urticaria; reduces allergic inflammation; injection reactions/anaphylaxis rare.
Antitussives	Suppress cough; examples: codeine, dextromethorphan; use mainly for dry irritating cough; avoid suppressing productive dangerous cough unnecessarily.
Mucolytics/expectorants	Reduce mucus viscosity or aid clearance; examples: acetylcysteine, ambroxol, guaifenesin.
H2 receptor antagonists	Block gastric H2 receptors and reduce acid secretion; example: famotidine; useful in GERD/ulcer disease though PPIs are stronger.
Famotidine	H2 receptor antagonist; reduces gastric acid secretion; fewer CYP interactions than cimetidine.
Sucralfate	Mucosal protectant forming a protective barrier over ulcers; requires acidic pH; can reduce absorption of other drugs.
H. pylori eradication therapy	Usually PPI plus antibiotics such as amoxicillin/clarithromycin/metronidazole depending local resistance; improves peptic ulcer cure and recurrence.
Laxative classes	Bulk-forming, osmotic, stimulant, and stool-softening agents; examples: psyllium, lactulose/macrogol, senna/bisacodyl, docusate.
IBD treatment overview	5-ASA for mild ulcerative colitis, corticosteroids for flares, immunosuppressants/biologics for maintenance or severe disease.
Insulin types classification	Rapid: lispro/aspart; short: regular; intermediate: NPH; long: glargine/detemir/degludec. Main risks: hypoglycemia and weight gain.
Oral antidiabetic classes	Main groups: metformin, sulfonylureas, meglitinides, TZDs, alpha-glucosidase inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 agonists.
Sulfonylureas - class	Close beta-cell KATP channels and increase insulin release; examples: gliclazide, glibenclamide; adverse effects: hypoglycemia, weight gain.
Meglitinides - class	Short-acting insulin secretagogues taken with meals; example: repaglinide; risk of hypoglycemia and weight gain.
Thiazolidinediones - class	PPAR-gamma agonists improving insulin sensitivity; example: pioglitazone; adverse effects: edema, weight gain, heart failure worsening, fractures.
Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors	Delay carbohydrate absorption in intestine; example: acarbose; adverse effects: flatulence, diarrhea, abdominal discomfort.
GLP-1 receptor agonists	Increase glucose-dependent insulin, slow gastric emptying, reduce appetite; examples: semaglutide, liraglutide; weight loss and GI effects.
DPP-4 inhibitors	Increase endogenous incretins; examples: sitagliptin, linagliptin; weight neutral, low hypoglycemia alone, pancreatitis warning.
SGLT2 inhibitors in diabetes	Increase urinary glucose excretion; examples: empagliflozin, dapagliflozin; benefits in selected CV/renal/HF patients; risks: genital infections, dehydration, euglycemic ketoacidosis.
Thyroid hormones	Levothyroxine is synthetic T4 for hypothyroidism; long half-life; absorption reduced by iron/calcium; overdose causes hyperthyroid symptoms.
Thionamides	Methimazole and propylthiouracil inhibit thyroid hormone synthesis; adverse effects: agranulocytosis, rash, hepatotoxicity.
Thyroid storm treatment	Emergency: beta-blocker, thionamide, iodine after thionamide, glucocorticoid, supportive care; PTU also reduces peripheral T4 to T3 conversion.
Combined oral contraceptives	Estrogen + progestin suppress ovulation; adverse effects: nausea, breast tenderness, VTE risk; contraindicated in high-risk thrombotic states.
Progestin-only contraception	Thickens cervical mucus and may suppress ovulation; useful when estrogen is contraindicated; irregular bleeding common.
Clomiphene	SERM that blocks estrogen feedback at hypothalamus, increasing GnRH/FSH/LH; used for ovulation induction.
Glucocorticoids - class	Anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive drugs; adverse effects: Cushingoid features, hyperglycemia, osteoporosis, infection risk, adrenal suppression.
Bisphosphonates - class	Inhibit osteoclast-mediated bone resorption; examples: alendronate, zoledronate; adverse effects: esophagitis, osteonecrosis of jaw rare.
Denosumab	Monoclonal antibody against RANKL; reduces osteoclast formation; osteoporosis treatment; risks: hypocalcemia, infections, rebound fractures if stopped abruptly.
Acute gout treatment	NSAIDs, colchicine, or corticosteroids; choice depends on renal function, GI risk, interactions, and contraindications.
Chronic gout prophylaxis	Urate-lowering therapy such as allopurinol/febuxostat; start with flare prophylaxis; allopurinol inhibits xanthine oxidase.
Classical anticancer drug groups	Main cytotoxic groups: alkylating agents, platinum compounds, antimetabolites, mitotic inhibitors, cytotoxic antibiotics/topoisomerase inhibitors.
Alkylating anticancer drugs	Crosslink DNA; example: cyclophosphamide; adverse effects: myelosuppression, infertility, hemorrhagic cystitis prevented by mesna/hydration.
Platinum anticancer drugs	Cisplatin/carboplatin/oxaliplatin crosslink DNA; toxicities include nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, neuropathy, severe nausea, myelosuppression depending drug.
Antimetabolite anticancer drugs	Interfere with DNA/RNA synthesis; examples: methotrexate, 5-FU, cytarabine; adverse effects: myelosuppression, mucositis.
Mitotic inhibitors	Vinca alkaloids inhibit microtubule polymerization; taxanes stabilize microtubules; main toxicities: neuropathy and myelosuppression depending drug.
Anthracyclines	Doxorubicin/daunorubicin intercalate DNA and inhibit topoisomerase II; major dose-limiting toxicity: cardiomyopathy; dexrazoxane may protect.
Calcineurin inhibitors	Cyclosporine and tacrolimus inhibit IL-2 transcription; transplant/autoimmune use; adverse effects: nephrotoxicity, hypertension, neurotoxicity.
Mycophenolate mofetil	Immunosuppressant inhibiting inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase and lymphocyte purine synthesis; adverse effects: GI upset, myelosuppression, teratogenicity.
Monoclonal antibodies - naming clue	-mab drugs are monoclonal antibodies; target selection determines use/toxicity, e.g. rituximab anti-CD20, trastuzumab anti-HER2, infliximab anti-TNF.
Immune checkpoint inhibitors	Anti-PD-1/PD-L1/CTLA-4 drugs enhance antitumor immunity; adverse effects are immune-related: colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis, endocrinopathies.
Anti-TNF drugs	Biologic immunosuppressants for RA/IBD/psoriasis; examples: infliximab, adalimumab, etanercept; screen for TB; infection risk.
IVIG	Pooled immunoglobulin used for immune deficiencies and autoimmune diseases; adverse effects: infusion reactions, thrombosis, renal dysfunction rarely.
Interferons - class	Cytokine immunomodulators used in some viral/malignant/immune diseases; adverse effects: flu-like symptoms, depression, myelosuppression.
